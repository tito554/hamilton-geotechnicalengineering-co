Hamilton’s seismic risk profile is shaped by its position within the stable craton of the Canadian Shield and the overlying glacial stratigraphy of the Lake Ontario basin. Although intraplate seismicity is moderate, the National Building Code of Canada (NBCC 2020) mandates site-specific hazard assessments for critical infrastructure on soft soils. This category addresses those demands through advanced ground motion characterization, integrating local shear-wave velocity profiles and basin-edge effects. For structures requiring enhanced resilience, base isolation seismic design decouples the superstructure from ground movement, while seismic microzonation maps the spatial variability of amplification across the city’s post-glacial sediments.

These studies are essential for hospitals, bridges, and industrial facilities where conventional spectra may underestimate long-period shaking. The presence of saturated fine-grained deposits in the Hamilton Harbour area elevates the importance of soil liquefaction analysis to prevent bearing capacity failure during a design earthquake. Our integrated workflow delivers compliant ground models that inform both foundation engineering and structural ductility requirements under NBCC provisions.
