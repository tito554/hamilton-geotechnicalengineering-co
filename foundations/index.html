In Hamilton, Ontario, the performance of any structure begins below grade, where complex glacial geology demands rigorous geotechnical assessment. Our foundations engineering services cover the full lifecycle, from initial subsurface characterization to design recommendations and construction verification. The region is underlain by the Halton Till, a dense, silty clay till overlying the Queenston Shale bedrock, with frequent interbeds of glaciolacustrine silt and sand. These deposits create variable bearing capacities and drainage conditions, while the deep shale valleys can introduce significant excavation challenges. All work is governed by the Ontario Building Code (OBC) and the Canadian Foundation Engineering Manual (CFEM), ensuring compliance with local safety factors for bearing resistance and settlement control.

A technically sound foundation design in Hamilton relies on a precise understanding of soil-structure interaction derived from high-quality site data. Our methodology integrates advanced In-Situ with comprehensive laboratory programs to define strength, compressibility, and hydraulic conductivity. We routinely employ the Cone Penetration Test (CPT) to delineate stratigraphy and estimate pile capacity in the glaciolacustrine deposits, while In-Situ such as pressuremeter tests provides modulus values for settlement analysis in the stiff till. Critical index properties, including Atterberg limits and grain size analysis via sieve and hydrometer, are determined in our accredited laboratory to classify the sensitive Leda-type clays occasionally encountered near the lake plain. These data streams are synthesized per CSA and ASTM standards to develop Limit States Design (LSD) parameters for shallow and deep foundations.

Hamilton’s diverse building stock requires tailored foundation solutions that account for challenging ground conditions prevalent in the urban core and waterfront redevelopment zones. For industrial expansions in the Bayfront area, deep foundations such as driven H-piles or drilled shafts are often socketed into Queenston Shale to bypass compressible fill and organic silts. In residential subdivisions near the escarpment, we address potential slope instability and frost heave susceptibility in the Halton Till through engineered granular pads and deepened footings. The adaptive reuse of heritage masonry structures downtown demands underpinning and micropile designs that limit vibration-induced settlement. A critical component of construction quality assurance is the field verification of backfill compaction, which we confirm using the field density test (sand cone method) to ensure engineered fill meets specified relative compaction, mitigating future differential settlement at the foundation interface.

Our process begins with a targeted investigation to define the ground model, proceeds through rigorous engineering analysis, and culminates in a comprehensive geotechnical report. Deliverables include bearing capacity charts, predicted total and differential settlements, lateral earth pressure diagrams for basement walls, and detailed construction specifications. By integrating local geological expertise with advanced testing, we provide foundation recommendations that directly reduce geotechnical uncertainty, optimize concrete and steel quantities, and protect against costly remedial work, delivering a reliable and verifiable foundation system for any project in the Hamilton region.
