Excavations in Hamilton, Ontario, demand a thorough understanding of the region's complex geology, which is dominated by the Niagara Escarpment and layered sedimentary bedrock of the Lockport Formation, overlying Queenston shale and glacial till deposits. Any subsurface project, from utility tunnels to deep foundations, must rigorously address the geotechnical challenges posed by variable overburden soils, karst features in the dolomitic limestone, and the need to manage groundwater infiltration. A successful excavation project begins with a comprehensive geotechnical investigation to characterize the site-specific stratigraphy and rock mass quality, ensuring compliance with the Ontario Building Code (OBC) and relevant Canadian Standards Association (CSA) guidelines for excavations and shoring.

The methodology for designing Excavations in Canada adheres to national standards, primarily CSA Z248 (Code for Tower Cranes) for related hoisting and CSA S16 for temporary steel structures, while the geotechnical design follows the principles outlined in the Canadian Foundation Engineering Manual. A critical component of the site characterization phase involves advanced In-Situ, such as CPT (Cone Penetration Test) to continuously profile soil stratigraphy and estimate geotechnical parameters in the overburden, complemented by rock coring and borehole televiewer surveys to assess fracture orientation and rock mass discontinuities. The In-Situ program is typically paired with a robust laboratory analysis of recovered samples to determine intact rock strength, Atterberg limits of cohesive soils, and the abrasivity of the rock for mechanized tunneling assessments.

Typical underground projects in Hamilton revolve around its aging infrastructure and industrial base, including combined sewer overflow (CSO) storage tunnels, watermain and trunk sewer rehabilitation, and the construction of deep building foundations and parking garages within the dense urban fabric of the lower city. For projects in areas with significant granular overburden, verifying the compaction of backfill and the integrity of the native ground is essential, often requiring a field density test (sand cone method) to ensure specified levels of compaction are achieved for temporary works platforms and permanent backfill around shafts. The proximity to Hamilton Harbour and numerous creeks also necessitates careful dewatering design to prevent base instability and control settlement in adjacent structures.

Our process for underground excavation design begins with a detailed geological model, built from targeted exploration and rigorous grain size analysis (sieve + hydrometer) to classify soils for effective stress analysis and filter design. The final deliverables include a Geotechnical Data Report and a comprehensive Excavation and Shoring Design Report, providing clear recommendations for excavation support methods, sequential excavation sequences, groundwater control measures, and a monitoring plan for ground movement. By integrating local geological expertise with a full suite of investigation and testing capabilities, we deliver a defensible design that mitigates risk, ensures worker safety, and achieves regulatory approval for your critical underground infrastructure in Hamilton.
